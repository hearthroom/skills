# Originality Adaptation

Use this when an author brings a canon or IP character, a fan premise, another
card, a copied draft, or a recognizable inspiration and wants a Hearthroom card
that stands as original work.

Adaptation is not a refusal to use inspiration. It turns inspiration into a new
engine:

```text
inspiration -> transferable fantasy -> original substitutions -> distance check -> packet
```

## Why it matters on Hearthroom

When a card is submitted for community review, reviewers see a similarity score
for the definition against other submitted and approved cards (the author's own
other cards are excluded). No threshold is documented. Treat originality of
`definition.md` as a real submission concern, not only a craft preference. See
`platform-facts.md`, Publishing.

## When to use

The author says "like X", "inspired by X", "based on this card", or "make this
original"; the draft carries exact names, quotes, scenes, outfits, lore terms,
factions, or visual symbols from a recognizable work; sample calibration flagged
copy risk. An author who explicitly wants a canon or fan card and confirms
allowed use keeps only the facts the scene needs and still avoids brittle trivia.

## Transform, do not rename

Do not copy exact names, quotes, scene text, outfits, proper nouns, faction
names, artifact names, route labels, tag strings, or distinctive compositions.

A draft is still too close if a reader can say: "same character, different
names"; "same setup, prop, reveal, and emotional beat"; "the summary and opening
only swap nouns"; "the voice uses the same catchphrase rhythm"; "the world keeps
the same unique rule with new labels".

## Transferable fantasy

| Keep | Transform |
|---|---|
| relationship asymmetry | new history, leverage, debt, taboo, or secret |
| player fantasy | new player role and first-scene pressure |
| emotional tension | new contradiction, cost, pacing gate, or boundary |
| voice function | new rhythm, vocabulary, tells, refusal style |
| world function | new rule, location logic, state, route costs |
| scene mechanic | new place, object, timer, witness, risk, or choice |

## Substitution axes

Change enough axes that the distance check passes; with recognisable material
that is usually five or more:

- role identity: job, social position, obligation, or mask
- player role: what the player knows, controls, risks, or can refuse
- relationship history: who owes, betrayed, rescued, hid, or misread what
- pressure source: timer, ritual, debt, public event, route lock, illness,
  invitation, inspection, missing object
- setting rule: what changes choices, not only the setting name
- symbolic object: what the player can touch, keep, break, return, or hide
- voice strategy: sentence rhythm, address terms, tells, what they say instead of the source's tic
- visual motif: silhouette, color, object, setting pressure, framing
- state labels: trust, debt, heat, clue, favor, suspicion, route, habit, risk
- opening proof: place, character action, player implication, second-turn move

## Distance check

- Could the card be pitched without naming the inspiration?
- Does the player role differ in what the player can decide or withhold?
- Does the first scene use a different place, object, timer, and pressure?
- Does the voice have a new rhythm and its own tells?
- Does the world or relationship state change through different labels?
- Would restoring the original names reveal the same scene?

If any answer shows renamed-copy risk, repair the substitutions before writing
fields.

## Real people and topical cards

Stay even-handed: every side gets its strongest case and its own pressure
move, and no side is the card's punchline. A real person may only say things
based on their real public statements, rewritten rather than quoted at length.
Never invent positions, private conduct or private relationships for them.
Check the public record before writing a line (`material-distillation.md`,
source hygiene); mark anything unverified as an assumption.

## Hand-off

Premise workshop for several directions; character core when the adapted role
is still a trope shell; relationship, world, scenario, daily-life, play,
generator, or ensemble skills when that engine needs a packet; visual identity
when the image direction is still too close; card author only after the distance
check passes.
