# Premise Workshop

Use this reference when the author has no settled card premise: only a mood,
trope, aesthetic, relationship fantasy, genre cluster, or a goal such as "make
it popular". Premise work happens before blueprinting. It opens choices, then
converges on one playable direction.

```text
taste signal -> contrast axes -> 3 directions -> pressure test -> packet
```

Do not start by writing fields. Do not run a long interview. If the author is
stuck, propose defaults and make the tradeoffs visible.

## Legibility gate

A premise may be strange or stylized, but a normal player must understand its
promise at a glance:

```text
recognizable contract -> human-scale first scene -> concrete player position ->
visible pressure -> novelty overlay
```

The recognizable contract is a shelf the player already knows: school
admission, survival, household visit, workplace duty, club activity, academy
training, investigation, travel, competition, shop or inn routine, mission,
tabletop adventure, simulator, generator, companion relationship. Put the
unusual idea on top of the shelf; never replace the shelf with an abstract
institution, metaphor or mood.

Five-second test: Where am I? Who is the character, in ordinary terms? Who am
I? What just happened or is about to happen? What can I do in the first reply?
If any answer needs a metaphor, a glossary or "you will understand later", the
direction is not ready.

## Route away

A concrete premise that needs fields: `hearthroom-card-author`. The card type
is the blocker: `hearthroom-archetype-director`. One weak layer is explicit:
the narrow skill for it. The author asks whether a draft is good enough:
`hearthroom-quality-auditor`.

## Questions and axes

Ask at most two questions; prefer assumptions plus options. Useful ones: which
feeling should survive every route (comfort, danger, intimacy, wonder, humor,
grief, power, mystery, care, rivalry, competence); how much the player defines
themselves (blank slate, lightly framed, specific position); whether romance is
central, optional, absent or a risky route; whether the strange element is
confirmed, ambiguous or mundane in disguise; whether the card feels like a
companion, an incident, a routine, a system, a world or a game; what is
off-limits in tone or intensity.

Choose three to five taste axes: warmth vs threat; everyday vs mythic;
relationship-first vs incident-first; blank-slate vs specific position;
comfort loop vs consequence loop; slow burn vs immediate pressure; ambiguity vs
explicit world rule; small setting vs wide world; quiet routine vs dramatic
event; playful system vs emotional scene; low intensity vs boundary-sensitive
intensity. Axes guide direction design; they are not labels.

## Direction grammar

Each direction differs by play contract, not only by aesthetics: title,
audience-legible pitch, primary contract (card type and shape), player
position, character seed, first scene, core loop, what changes after the first
reply, involvement ladder, tradeoff or risk, best next skill. When the author
wants player freedom, the involvement ladder names a distant route, a
practical route, a curiosity route, an intimacy or trust route, and a conflict
or refusal route. Every rung continues play.

## Pressure test

Pick one or two tension sources and name the player's pressure function:

| Source | Works when | Player pressure function |
|---|---|---|
| Secret | knowledge changes access, trust or risk | hide, expose, test, bargain |
| Debt | someone owes or is owed | collect, forgive, delay, transfer |
| Timer | time changes state or closes a route | choose what to risk before the deadline |
| Boundary | a line can be crossed, defended or renegotiated | set terms, push back |
| Resource | something scarce affects future choices | spend, save, steal, trade |
| Social risk | public attention creates cost | cover, accuse, perform, withdraw |
| Rule | a world or system rule creates consequence | exploit, obey, break, reveal |
| Care cost | helping one thing neglects another | choose who or what changes |

Then test the direction:

- Can a player describe the premise to a friend without private lore?
- Does the direction name a recognizable shelf before its novelty layer?
- Can the first player reply be written in under ten seconds?
- Does the player have leverage beyond admiring, comforting or obeying?
- What changes after the first reply?
- Which field will carry the durable rules: definition, Lorebook, opening or
  output contract?
- What is the biggest failure mode: generic trope, lore dump, forced intimacy,
  passive character, fake choices, length bloat or unclear rating?
- Funnel check: L0, would the title and a cover alone make a stranger stop?
  L1, does a summary of about 260 characters lead straight into the first
  scene? L2, is the opening a free demo: voice heard, one low-friction first
  action, a pull to turn two? L3, does turn two beat turn one? Funnel L0–L3
  and "weakest layer first": `role-card-writing-framework.md`.

Recommend the direction whose weakest funnel layer is strongest, with the
clearest first reply and the best second-turn engine, not the largest lore or
prettiest mood. Reject abstract prestige: a
beautifully named place, object or ritual that does not tell the player what
kind of story this is. Replace it with a human-scale situation first.

## Common repairs

| Failure | Repair |
|---|---|
| Vibe only | convert mood into player position, pressure and first scene |
| "Popular card" request | define craft goals; attach no performance claims |
| Too many genres | one primary contract; the rest become overlays |
| Romance assumed | define romance as central, optional, absent or gated |
| Mystery without leverage | give the player a clue, access, suspicion or choice |
| Cozy becomes no action | add a tiny disruption, shared object or small cost |
| Eerie becomes shock | tie strangeness to motive, route or consequence |
| Light fantasy becomes lore dump | keep one rule and show it through action |
| Player freedom becomes blankness | define player controls and route options |
| Abstract institution dominates | translate it into a job, school, mission, survival problem or investigation |
| First scene is atmosphere only | begin with a person doing something to the player, a door to enter, a threat to answer or a route to choose |

"Top card" translates into: a clear promise, strong player agency, memorable
character behaviour, a consequential second turn, length discipline, and an
original premise, since the definition's originality is reviewed on submission.
