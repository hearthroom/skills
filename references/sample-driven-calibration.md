# Sample-Driven Calibration

Use this when an author or agent wants examples, golden samples, or a comparison
between a draft and the toolkit's synthetic samples.

Calibration is not copying. It compares craft structure:

```text
sample shape -> draft shape -> missing craft layer -> repair skill -> original rewrite
```

## Source posture

Use only the synthetic samples in `examples/sample-card-packets.md`. They are
fictional teaching fixtures; attach no origin or performance claims to them.

Do not copy sample names, scene text, voice lines, tag strings, or world details
into a real card. Borrow the contract shape and checklist logic: field
allocation, tension shape, first-screen proof, probe design, repair order.

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Ask |
|---|---|
| Card shape | Is the primary contract as clear as the sample's? |
| Promise surface | Do name, summary, and tags explain the player position and loop? |
| Durable engine | Does the definition carry reusable behavior, routes, state, boundaries? |
| Opening proof | Does the opening prove the promise through action, pressure, reply paths? |
| Voice proof | Do examples show rhythm, motive, refusal style, pressure behavior? |
| Longplay proof | Are state, memory, route costs, or artifact continuity visible? |
| Allocation | Are durable rules in the definition and immediate play in the opening? |
| Test hooks | Are play probes tied to expected failures and repairs? |
| Originality | Can you explain what structure was borrowed without reusing text? |

## Sample selection

Pick the closest one or two: relationship (slow-burn companion, guarded
intimacy, repair and refusal routes); daily-life (routine, tiny disruption,
shared object, quiet progression); story or scenario (incident, clues, routes,
false leads, consequences); game (compact state, resources, turn protocol,
failure-forward choices); generator (artifact contract, intake defaults, output
schema, revision operations).

Choose a primary sample and at most one overlay. Do not average samples into a
hybrid.

## Copy-risk check

Flag when the draft reuses: names, locations, artifacts, factions, or proper
nouns; opening sentences, dialogue rhythms, or distinctive images; the exact tag
set or summary structure with nouns swapped; route names, resource labels, or
schema labels.

Repair by keeping the craft intent and changing the material: new relation, new
pressure source, new object, new state labels, new first-screen proof.

## Good and bad calibration

Good: "Borrow the allocation: compact promise, durable engine in the definition,
immediate pressure in the opening." "Keep the same probe type: refusal route and
second-turn consequence." "Do not reuse the rain-door scene; create a different
pressure object and history."

Bad: "Use this sample as a template and swap names." "This is based on
successful cards." "Just make it longer and more emotional."
