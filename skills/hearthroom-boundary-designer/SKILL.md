# Hearthroom Boundary Designer

Design controlled intensity that never steals player agency. Boundaries are
mechanics tied to motive, consequence and choice, not disclaimers. The
output is a boundary packet and repair plan, not a card.

## Required references

Read `../../references/boundary-design.md` (agency contract, intensity and
explicitness, pressure tools, escalation ladder, refusal sequence, safer
fallback, real people and topical premises, first-scene guardrails, probes,
repair map) and `../../references/cost-and-boundaries.md`.

## Workflow

1. Name the pressure shape: adult romance, jealousy, coercion, power
   imbalance, horror, violence, trauma, obsession, scandal, boundary repair.
2. Settle intended intensity and the explicitness ceiling; when unclear,
   default lower and mark the question for the author. Every central
   character is clearly adult when the card is adult or romantic. The
   ceiling is a level the card steers within through the story: no explicit
   sexual text in any field, and the card never restricts the player. It
   must agree with the rating questionnaire answered at submission.
3. Write the player agency contract (controls, can refuse, the card must
   never decide), the allowed pressure tools and the disallowed moves.
4. Build the escalation ladder with gate and slowdown signals per level, and
   the in-character refusal, slowdown and stop behaviour: acknowledge, stop,
   preserve motive, offer a new route, never a lecture. A real person says
   only what their public statements support; a topical premise keeps every
   side's strongest case.
5. Add a safer fallback that keeps the fantasy at lower pressure, and
   first-scene guardrails: a reason to interact, visible exits or terms, one
   pressure, one softening action, reply paths that include refusal.
6. Write play probes for `hearthroom-chat-simulation`.

## Packet

Intended intensity, ceiling, premise risk; agency contract, pressure tools,
disallowed moves; escalation ladder, refusal and stop behaviour, safer
fallback; first-scene guardrails and probes; repair plan (definition rules,
opening changes, refusal style, character tradeoff); missing author input.
Durable rules go in `definition.md`, visible guardrails in `welcome.md`.
Continue with `hearthroom-card-blueprint` or `hearthroom-card-author`;
`hearthroom-instruction-guardrail` only when play shows the fields cannot
hold a refusal style.

## Do not

- Do not let "no" end the card; refusal opens another route.
- Do not let a visible opening contradict hidden boundary rules; rewrite the
  opening.
- Do not use validation output as the judge of emotional safety.
