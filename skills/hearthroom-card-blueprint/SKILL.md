# Hearthroom Card Blueprint

The design stage between a direction and the files. A blueprint settles the
decisions a card cannot leave open (who the player is, what the character
wants, what the first screen does, what turn two does) so that writing the
fields is assembly, not invention. No files and no CLI from here.

## Workflow

1. Restate the seed in one sentence and name the missing inputs: player role,
   relationship pressure, card shape, content rating intent, language,
   success criteria. Where a packet is missing, get it from its skill first
   (premise workshop, material distiller, boundary designer, archetype
   director, series architect, ensemble director, or the shape skill).
2. If the seed is vague, propose two or three directions that differ in kind
   (player role, conflict, first scene, loop, long-term consequence), name
   the obvious one and say why the chosen one is more playable. Preserve a
   premise packet's selected direction instead of re-brainstorming.
3. Build the tension triangle: role desire, player leverage, external
   pressure.
4. Define the character core: identity, desire, contradiction, boundary,
   mask or wound, pressure behaviour, what changes as the player moves
   closer or pushes back. Appearance only where it deviates from the
   default picture (hide the name: still recognisable?). Relationship to the
   player as one concrete image, never "deep feelings".
5. Define the player insertion space: what the player controls, can refuse,
   can change, and what the card must never decide.
6. Build the engine for the shape (relationship, world, play, generator,
   scenario, daily life); define the world only as far as it creates play,
   with sometimes-needed facts as Lorebook entries.
7. Define the voice fingerprint: rhythm, vocabulary, address terms, tells,
   refusal style, and "says instead" for any tic to remove; a contrast
   matrix for ensembles.
8. Decide allocation: definition, opening, Lorebook, output contract,
   display rules.
9. Design the first scene and the second-turn engine together. The opening
   is the free demo: voice heard, one low-friction first action, a pull to
   answer; the second-turn move is better than the opening, not a
   restatement. Write the expected first user message.
10. Draft a compact summary, a definition outline and an opening concept.
    Get a `hearthroom-quality-auditor` scorecard first only when the author
    asked for an audit.

## Decisions to keep explicit

Keep these in the dossier or your reply; the shape is a checklist, not a
form: recommended direction and why; tension triangle; character core;
player insertion; engine packet(s); voice fingerprint; first scene and
second-turn engine; state fields and visibility; `uiRole` and the status
overhead threshold; which rule joins the iron rules and the recency
checklist; allocation; boundary packet when sensitive; what still needs
clarifying before authoring. Preserve every narrow packet by name and
content; do not summarise away cast decisions, reply paths, state fields,
route costs or the second-turn move. Then continue with
`hearthroom-card-author`.

## Do not

- Do not pass on a card where the player only watches, or one that is still
  a genre label ("vampire", "academy", "RPG").
- Do not copy unprovided material or claim an origin you cannot support.
