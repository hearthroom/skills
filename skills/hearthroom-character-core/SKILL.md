# Character Core

Give the character an engine instead of a biography. The output is a
character-core packet the next skill carries into `definition.md`; it does
not touch the card folder.

## Required references

Read `../../references/character-core-design.md`. Read
`../../references/relationship-engine.md` when the character is clear but
the relationship drifts into flirting or comfort, and
`../../references/boundary-design.md` for mature, jealous, power-imbalanced
or consent-sensitive pressure.

## Workflow

1. Name the failure: trope-only, mood-only, biography-only, passive,
   interchangeable in an ensemble, no player leverage, no boundary, no
   contradiction, weak motive or an unplayable secret. Restate the seed in
   one sentence without adding fields.
2. Choose one to three appeal axes and convert each into behaviour with the
   transforms table: "cold" becomes a refusal style, a soft spot and a
   pressure move.
3. Build the chain: desire, contradiction, boundary, mask or wound, player
   leverage, pressure behaviour. A missing link is where the character
   drifts into generic friendliness or exposition.
4. Define asymmetry (who knows, needs, owes, risks, controls, hides or can
   lose what) and hooks that give the player something to do besides
   admire, comfort or wait; fill the reply-path matrix rows (trusts,
   questions, resists, passive, sets a boundary, breaks trust) so refusal
   opens an alternate route.
5. Keep only the appearance that deviates from the default picture (hide
   the name: still recognisable?) and delete any backstory line the
   character would act the same without.
6. Say where each part lands: summary sells appeal and tension; definition
   keeps the chain, tells and pressure rows; the opening starts where the
   core becomes actionable; one ordinary-turn sample in this voice. Name
   which rule joins the top iron rules and its line in the recency checklist.

## Packet

Failure; appeal promise; identity; desire / contradiction / boundary; mask
or wound; player leverage; asymmetry; pressure behaviour per row; soft spots
and hard limits; tells and deviating appearance; hooks; field targets.
Continue with `hearthroom-card-blueprint`, `hearthroom-relationship-architect`
for pacing and repair, `hearthroom-voice-director` for speech or ensemble
contrast, or `hearthroom-card-author`.

## Do not

- Do not stop at labels such as shy, cold, gentle or mysterious; turn each
  into behaviour under pressure.
- Do not solve a thin character with more biography.
- Do not borrow real people or existing cards.
