# Collaboration director

Turn the author's reactions into observable card behaviour and a decision,
then route to the narrow skill that makes the patch. The one record is the
card's `README.md` dossier (rejected directions, decisions, evidence by
version), which is never sent. The output is a decision frame, not a
rewrite and not a file edit.

## Required references

Read the card's dossier, then `../../references/author-collaboration.md`.
Read `../../references/platform-facts.md` before describing what a push,
render or paid turn does. Read a follow-up reference only when the
decision needs it: `../../references/premise-workshop.md` for unsettled
direction, `../../references/card-diagnosis.md` for mixed symptoms,
`../../references/quality-scorecard.md` for a whole-card craft decision or
a version comparison, `../../references/playtest-loop.md` for play
evidence, `../../references/presentation-design.md` for "too busy".

## Workflow

1. Identify what the author is reacting to: idea, draft, one file, rendered
   opening, play reply, two versions, profile, language or readiness.
2. Separate evidence (what happened) from preference (what they want more or
   less of), and translate the feedback into behaviour: pressure, voice,
   agency, pacing, token density, relationship distance, opening affordance,
   route consequence, boundary posture, profile promise, Story/UI balance.
3. Name non-negotiables and what to preserve before proposing changes.
4. When direction is unsettled, offer two or three options with what each
   changes, preserves and risks. When two versions are compared, show them
   on the same probes and model, layer by layer, and say when the difference
   is within the noise of one run.
5. Recommend one path with the tradeoff in card-design terms. Ask at most
   three targeted questions, only when the decision cannot be made from the
   evidence at hand.
6. Write rejected directions and the decision into the dossier with reasons,
   then continue with the narrow skill: premise workshop, archetype director
   or blueprint for concept choices; card doctor for several symptoms;
   quality auditor for a whole-card decision; profile packager, language
   stylist, presentation director or sandbox kit for those layers; render
   review or chat simulation for evidence; card author when the decision is
   ready to become file edits.

## Do not

- Do not rewrite on "boring" or answer "not like the character" with style
  polish; locate the weakness and route it.
- Do not average two incompatible drafts; preserve the winning qualities.
- Do not replace the author's taste with your own, and do not call a
  difference between two versions an improvement on one reply.
