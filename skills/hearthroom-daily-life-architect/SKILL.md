# Hearthroom Daily-Life Architect

Give a quiet card a playable engine: a repeatable routine with a small
desire, a tiny disruption, a shared object, habit state, player leverage and
renewed hooks. Quiet is not empty. The output is a daily-life packet; most
daily-life cards use `type` `companion`.

## Required references

Read `../../references/daily-life-design.md`. Read the narrow reference only
when it is the blocker: `../../references/character-core-design.md` (no
small desire), `../../references/opening-design.md` (the first routine
moment), `../../references/longplay-design.md` (routine memory across
sessions), `../../references/agency-design.md` (the player can only watch or
comfort), `../../references/relationship-engine.md` (romance pacing),
`../../references/voice-calibration.md` (quiet restraint drifts),
`../../references/talk-example-design.md` (the ordinary-turn sample).
When relationship state is the main blocker use
`hearthroom-relationship-architect`; when an incident drives the card,
`hearthroom-scenario-architect`.

## Workflow

1. State the player role, the ordinary routine, the small playable desire
   and the tiny disruption, and the reason the moment starts now.
2. Choose one shared object or place that can return changed later, and the
   sensory anchors.
3. Build the loop: routine, disruption, player choice, small state change,
   the next routine returns altered. Pick the micro-tension (privacy, time,
   competence, boundary, memory, social friction, care cost) and the compact
   habit state.
4. Design at least three reply paths that change habit, object, trust,
   distance, boundary, mood, promise or next routine differently (the player
   can help, refuse, ask, notice, fix, tease, leave, set terms or reorder),
   plus passive-player behaviour that continues through action tied to the
   routine.
5. State the romance posture (friendship-first, slow-burn optional,
   non-romantic, cohabitation friction, or another).
6. Design the opening moment, the expected first player message, the
   second-turn change and the return-next-time hooks.
7. Allocate fields: one ordinary-turn sample at the card's usual closeness,
   never the confession; which rule joins the iron rules and the recency
   checklist; play probes.

## Packet

Promise; player role; routine; small desire; tiny disruption; shared object
or place; sensory anchors; loop; micro-tension; habit state; reply paths;
passive-player behaviour; romance posture; opening moment; expected first
message; second-turn change; long-session renewal; field allocation; probes.
Continue with `hearthroom-card-blueprint` when character core or voice is
still open, `hearthroom-card-author` when coherent, `hearthroom-opening-director`
when the first scene is still mood prose, or `hearthroom-chat-simulation`
after push.

## Do not

- Do not repair quietness with melodrama unless the author asked for drama.
- Do not force romance; state the posture instead.
