# Premise Workshop

The earliest creative stage. The output is a premise packet: three
contrasted playable directions and one recommendation. Not final fields;
it does not touch the card folder.

## Required references

Read `../../references/premise-workshop.md`. Read
`../../references/archetype-contracts.md` when the directions need a
primary-contract comparison.

## Workflow

1. Restate the author's seed as a taste brief. Continue only if no
   character, player position, first scene or primary contract is settled;
   a concrete premise that needs fields goes to `hearthroom-card-author`,
   one explicit weak layer to its narrow skill.
2. Ask at most two questions, only when the missing choice blocks every
   useful direction; otherwise state assumptions and proceed.
3. Name three to five taste axes, then propose exactly three directions that
   differ by primary contract, player position, first scene, core loop and
   risk, not only by aesthetics: the safest, the boldest, and the most
   unusual whose first reply still passes the five-second test. Say what the
   obvious card would be and why each direction is not it.
4. Pressure-test each: first reply clarity, player leverage, what changes
   after the first reply, likely failure mode, best next skill, and the
   funnel (L0 cover and title, L1 summary, L2 opening as a free demo, L3
   turn two better than turn one). Repair any direction that fails the
   five-second gate.
5. Recommend the direction whose weakest layer is strongest and say why it
   is more playable than the generic version; recommend even when the
   author is stuck. Rejected ideas go to the card's `README.md` so a later
   session does not reopen them.

## Packet

Seed; taste signals; assumptions and questions asked; taste axes; three
directions (title, pitch, primary contract, player position, character seed,
first scene, core loop, what changes after the first reply, involvement
ladder, risk, best next skill); recommendation and why; rejected ideas;
risk flags; next decisions to lock. Continue with
`hearthroom-archetype-director` to choose the type,
`hearthroom-card-blueprint` for full planning, or the layer skill that
blocks the chosen direction.

## Do not

- Do not let a poetic institution, object, ritual or mood stand in for a
  premise; attach the novelty to a familiar shelf.
- Do not turn "popular" into performance claims; translate it into craft
  goals.
- Do not assume romance is central unless the author says so.
