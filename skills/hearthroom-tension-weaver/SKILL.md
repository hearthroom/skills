# Tension Weaver

Build the pressure the player can answer: what the character wants, what
the player can change, and why the scene starts now. The output is a
tension packet; it does not touch the card folder.

## Required references

Read `../../references/tension-triangle.md`. Read
`../../references/character-core-design.md` when character desire or
boundary is missing and `../../references/agency-design.md` when the player
has no real control.

## Workflow

1. Name the inertness: missing character desire, player leverage, external
   pressure, why-now, consequence or renewal. No direction yet goes to
   `hearthroom-premise-workshop`; a weak character core, agency, opening or
   one specialised engine goes to that skill instead.
2. Pick one or two tension sources (`premise-workshop.md`, "Pressure test")
   and build the triangle: desire that creates action, leverage that
   changes consequence, external pressure with a visible first-scene form.
   Prefer concrete objects, deadlines, debts, secrets, rules, social risks
   or care costs over abstract destiny.
3. Write one first-scene hook and one why-now statement a single opening
   beat can prove, and the consequence if the player does nothing.
4. Fill the reply-path rows (accepts, questions, refuses or sets terms,
   redirects or exploits, does nothing); each leads to a different state,
   route, access, risk, relationship, clue or boundary outcome.
5. Say how the pressure escalates or changes form by turn three, and where
   each part lands (summary, definition, opening, examples).

Continue with `hearthroom-card-blueprint` when the triangle is ready for
full planning, `hearthroom-opening-director` when only the first screen is
weak, `hearthroom-character-core` or `hearthroom-agency-designer` when
desire or leverage is still missing, or the engine skill that should carry
the pressure.

## Do not

- Do not fix inertness with prettier mood prose, a larger lore dump or
  forced player feelings.
